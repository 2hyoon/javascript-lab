Tab Component

A set of panels that share one area of the page, with only one panel shown at a time. Pick a tab with the mouse, or move through them with the arrow keys.

What is a tab?

A tab widget lets several panels of related content share the same space. The tabs act as labels for those panels, so a reader can see what is available without scrolling past all of it, and only the selected panel is rendered at any one time.

Each button carries aria-controls pointing at the panel it owns, and each panel points back with aria-labelledby. That pairing is what tells a screen reader which heading belongs to which tab.

Keyboard support

The whole tab strip is a single stop in the tab order. Once focus lands there, the left and right arrow keys move between tabs and wrap around at either end, while Home and End jump straight to the first and last tab.

That is a roving tabindex: the selected tab holds tabindex="0" and every other tab holds -1, so pressing Tab leaves the strip instead of walking through every label.

When not to use tabs

Tabs hide everything except the panel in front, so they are a poor fit when the reader needs to compare two panels, or when the content is meant to be read straight through. Hidden text is also missed by the browser's find-in-page.

If the sections are independent and the reader may want several open at once, an accordion is usually the better shape.

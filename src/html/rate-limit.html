Debounce vs Throttle

Both cut a stream of events down to fewer calls, which is where the confusion starts — the shapes they produce have nothing in common. Move the pointer across the pad, or hold a key while it has focus, and watch the lanes fill in.

  • 0 raw
  • 0 debounce
  • 0 throttle · trailing
  • 0 throttle · leading
  • 0 requestAnimationFrame
300 ms

What to look for

  • Keep moving without stopping. The debounce lane stays empty the whole time — it is waiting for you to stop. Both throttle lanes keep firing at a steady beat.
  • Now stop. One debounce mark appears, wait ms after your last event. That is the entire difference between the two ideas.
  • Move the pointer once, then leave it alone. leading fires instantly; trailing fires wait ms later, landing on top of the debounce mark. A single isolated event cannot tell trailing throttle and debounce apart.
  • Start a burst and watch the front edge. leading marks the very first event; trailing is one full wait behind it for the whole run. At the back edge they swap: trailing lands a final mark after you stop, leading does not.
  • The gridlines are one second apart, so you can count either throttle interval against them and check it matches wait.
  • Now drag the wait slider while moving. Every lane changes its spacing except requestAnimationFrame — that one is not on your clock, it is on the display's. Count its marks between two gridlines and you have read your refresh rate: 60 on most screens, 120 on ProMotion.

Autocomplete

Every keystroke could be a request, and the answers do not have to come back in the order they were asked. Type quickly and the debounce keeps most of them from ever leaving; type slowly and the toggles below decide whether a late answer is allowed to overwrite a newer one.

    Network simulation

      What to look for

      • Type a word at speed. The log shows one request, not one per letter — the debounce waits for you to stop.
      • Now type one letter every half second. Each new request cancels the one before it, and the cancelled ones never reach the list.
      • Turn on Hold each response back and type slowly. The answers now arrive out of order, and the list still only ever shows the newest query. Cancelling is half of that; the other half is that a response which had already fully arrived cannot be cancelled at all, so it is checked against the query and dropped on the way in.
      • Turn off Discard stale responses and repeat. The list now flickers between answers to words you have already finished typing. That is the race condition, and it is the entire reason for both mechanisms above.
      • Put the pointer away. ↓ and ↑ walk the list, Enter takes one, Esc closes the list and then clears the field. Focus never leaves the text box — the highlight moves through aria-activedescendant, because a list that stole focus would stop you typing.
